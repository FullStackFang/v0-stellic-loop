On you: what your groups need from you

One list across the groups you belong to, soonest first. Each row says the kind (Vote, Show up, Do), what it is, which group, and when, and most are answered right in the row. Everything below is clickable. Pick where the list sits, then say what you think of the alert, the organizer's page and how a group is asked.

1The list, behind a counter on the campus screen

Try Done, a vote and I'm in: the row leaves and the count drops. Use the switch to see the screen with nothing waiting.

CBehind a counter in the top bar (picked)

The week is untouched. A counter in the bar says how many things are on you and opens the list over the screen (shown open here; press the counter to close it). With nothing on you the counter is gone. The row of events always shows whole tickets, never a cut one, and scrolling snaps to the next set: use the round arrows.

2"This is yours": work someone put on you

When a teammate proposes a piece of work for you, it is not a row. It is handed to you: a card drops in from the top corner, the person's picture pulses twice, and it waits for one of two answers. It stays until you answer and is back on your next visit if you ignore it. One at a time, oldest first.

3The organizer's page for one to-do

What the person who posted it, and the group's owners and organizers, see. Nobody needs to be the site admin. Who has not done it is a list with a name and an email, in a frame that scrolls. The reminder names nobody. Moving the date works before or after it has passed, and the people already done stay done. The same page serves a poll.

4Asking a group, from the campus screen

Opened by "Ask a group" at the top of the list, which only people who may post in a group see. It is one line, like posting a plan: Loopd reads the group, the kind and the date out of the sentence and shows what it understood. Edit the line and watch the reading change. The "+" on the week still means one thing: post a plan.

    5How it reaches you

    Most people will not open Loopd to look at a list. These are the three ways the same items come to you, on a phone. The first two exist today for votes; the third is new and is not in the written plan yet. Tap through each.

    AA link in the group chat

    The organizer posts once and drops the link where the group already talks. You tap it, land on that one item, answer, and leave. Tap the link below.

    BYour assistant tells you

    For anyone who has connected one. At the start of a conversation it says what is waiting, with the same rows and buttons, then gets on with what you asked.

    COne message a week

    New. Monday morning, one email with everything on you across your groups, soonest first, and nothing else. It replaces a separate email per item. Each button answers without opening Loopd.
