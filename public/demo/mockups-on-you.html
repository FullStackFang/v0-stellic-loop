On you: what your groups need from you

One list across the groups you belong to, soonest first. Each row says the kind (Vote, Show up, Do), what it is, which group, and when, and most are answered right in the row. Everything below is clickable. Pick where the list sits, then say what you think of the alert, the organizer's page and how a group is asked.

1Where the list sits on the campus screen

The campus screen is one full-height view, so the list has to take its room from somewhere. The rows are identical in all three; only the place differs. Try Done, a vote and I'm in: the row leaves and the count drops. Use the switch to see each screen with nothing waiting.

AA column beside the week

Always in view, on the right. The week keeps its row of tickets and gives up about a third of its width. With nothing on you the column is gone and the week has the whole screen.

BA strip above the week

Always in view, across the top, scrolling sideways the way the tickets do. It keeps the week full width and costs it height. With nothing on you the strip is gone.

CBehind a counter in the top bar

The week is untouched. A counter in the bar says how many things are on you and opens the list over the screen (shown open here). With nothing on you the counter is gone. The cost: you only see what is waiting if you look.

2"This is yours": work someone put on you

When a teammate proposes a piece of work for you, it is not a row. It is handed to you: a card drops in from the top corner, the person's picture pulses twice, and it waits for one of two answers. It stays until you answer and is back on your next visit if you ignore it. One at a time, oldest first.

3The organizer's page for one to-do

What the person who posted it, and the group's owners and organizers, see. Nobody needs to be the site admin. Who has not done it is a list with a name and an email, in a frame that scrolls. The reminder names nobody. Moving the date works before or after it has passed, and the people already done stay done. The same page serves a poll.

4Asking a group, from the campus screen

Opened by "Ask a group" at the top of the list, which only people who may post in a group see. It is one line, like posting a plan: Loop reads the group, the kind and the date out of the sentence and shows what it understood. Edit the line and watch the reading change. The "+" on the week still means one thing: post a plan.
