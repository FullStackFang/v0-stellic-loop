Drop an intent · free text + @ pins

You write whatever you want. Structure is opt-in: type @ and Loop offers what people near you already want (what · day · how often · where · time), ranked by demand — or pin your own word. Pinned things are what fires matching; every other word travels with the intent as your note, so nothing you type is thrown away. Opens with the most popular recent drop as a ghost you can take with Tab or ignore.

01

@ mentions, inline

recommended
  • Slack-style: type freely; @ opens the ranked picker; the pick lands as an inline chip in your sentence. Everything else stays plain text.
  • Words Loop recognises without an @ (dinner, Fridays, weekly) show underneath as dashed "read from your words" pills — it understood you, you didn't have to do anything. Tap a chip to swap it.
  • No known activity? It still drops — routed as pending for a human read, exactly like the You-tab quick-add today.

Try: Tab to take the ghost · type @ · type @ram → pin your own · type with people who like spicy food.

02

Plain textarea, @ inserts text

  • Nothing fancy in the field — it's a normal textarea. @ still opens the ranked picker, but the pick is inserted as text (@Collegetown), like the Floor's tag input.
  • "What Loop read" underneath is the only structure you see; dashed + day / + often pills are tap-to-fill gaps.
  • Least surprising, least code (reuses parseQuickIntent). Slightly less legible which words are pinned.

Try: type jog fridays, then @ → Collegetown; tap + how often.

03

Auto-chips as you type, @ for extras

  • Like 01, but known words become chips the moment you finish typing them (dinner␣ → chip). @ is only for things it wouldn't guess (places, your own words).
  • Fastest to a structured intent; the sentence visibly "locks in" as you write.
  • Trade-off: chips appearing under your fingers can feel presumptuous; needs a clean way to un-chip (backspace does it).

Try: type dinner thursdays weekly slowly and watch; then @ → a place.
