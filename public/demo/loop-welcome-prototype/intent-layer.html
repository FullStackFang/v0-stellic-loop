What you want should have a life beyond one chat.

You’ve already explained the kind of day you enjoy. The thing you want to learn. What would make you say yes.

Then comes a new chat, a new group, another event. You piece the background together again.

Loop gives the wants and preferences you choose to save a place of their own. Connected assistants can use them alongside the resources and opportunities in your loops.

So a passing “I’d love to” can become a suggestion that fits you, a plan with other people, or a better starting point next time.

You choose what carries forward, who can use it, and when access ends.

Why this is worth building.

Research supports the need. Loop’s benefits still need to be measured in use.

Earlier preferences don’t reliably shape later answers.

PrefEval tested 3,000 preference/query pairs across 10 models. In its zero-shot setting, most tested models fell below 10% preference-following accuracy after 10 turns. This is evidence from specific models and a 2025 benchmark, not a failure rate for all current assistants. Zhao et al., ICLR 2025.

Loop’s hypothesis: making relevant, user-reviewed intent available at the next question can reduce repeated explanation. The pilot must compare this with an assistant’s existing memory.

Useful memory needs to keep up with change.

LongMemEval tests updating knowledge, reasoning across sessions and time, extracting information, and knowing when an answer is unavailable. Its 500 questions expose challenges beyond simply storing a long chat history. Wu et al., ICLR 2025.

Loop’s design response: distinguish lasting preferences from a plan for this week, and let corrections and feedback change what is used next.

A concrete next step can help an intention become action.

In a randomized study of 3,272 employees, prompting people to write a vaccination date and time increased vaccination by 4.2 percentage points, adjusted for covariates. Milkman et al., PNAS 2011. Another randomized study of 877 gym members found no positive effect of simple planning prompts on gym attendance. Carrera et al., 2018.

That makes follow-through a question to test, not a promise to assume. Neither study evaluates Loop’s group commitments.

What this concept depends on

Context comes from what you choose to save and the loops you can access. Connecting an assistant is required; Loop does not automatically read every chat or group. The proposed data boundary keeps compiled wants and intents rather than raw conversations. Revoking access stops future reads; it cannot erase information an assistant has already received.
