The four scroll rules

All four are cheap in the real app. They turn "the card got cut off" into "there is more below", which is the only honest thing a bottom edge can say.

RuleWhat it doesIn code
1 · ClearanceThe list ends with tab bar + 24px + 16px of padding, so the last card's "open loop" corner is never under the bar, even at the very bottom.padding-bottom: calc(var(--tabbar) + 40px) on the list (index.html uses pb-28 today, same idea).
2 · Fade, not a lineA 36px gradient sits above the tab bar. A card passing under it dissolves, so a sliced card reads as scrollable rather than broken.One absolutely positioned .fade element, pointer-events:none.
3 · One open at a timeOpening a row folds the previous card. Home stays scannable and the total height is bounded, so cut-offs are rare to begin with.Accordion state: open = id, not a set. Easy to flip later if people want several open.
4 · Bring it into viewAfter a card opens, it scrolls so its bottom clears the bar (or its top aligns if it is taller than the view). Folding keeps your scroll position.scrollIntoView({ block:'nearest' }) plus scroll-padding-bottom and scroll-margin so the browser accounts for the floating bar.
