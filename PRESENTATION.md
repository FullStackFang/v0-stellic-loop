## Slide 2 · Thesis + aspiration

**Thesis, student side**
> Loop gives a first-year student a small recurring crew to show up with for the campus activities they already meant to attend.

**Thesis, institution side**
> Loop gives a student-engagement director a live, behavioral measure of which first-years are actually participating in campus life.

(Each: one offering, one segment, one benefit. No "and", no "if".)

**Aspiration**
> A campus where every student has people they show up with — and no one drifts through their first year unseen.

## Section 3 · Customer segments (2 slides)

**Slide 3 · Student side**
- Segment (index-card): *First-year, on-campus undergraduates at a residential
  four-year university.*
  - Outwardly perceivable: class year, housing status, .edu enrollment.
  - Homogeneous need: new, socially unanchored, deciding in the first weeks
    whether campus is "theirs."
  - Findable: through the residence-hall floor, the intro course, the club
    fair.
- Archetype (end user): **Sarah Chen** — Class of '29, Donlon Hall, in CS 2110.
  Three weeks in, she means to go to the club fair follow-ups, the CS study
  nights, the dorm events — and goes to none, because going alone is
  uncomfortable and no plan ever firms up. Campus feels big and anonymous; she
  leaves for her room right after class.

**Slide 4 · Institution side (beachhead)**
- Segment (index-card): *Student-engagement / campus-activities offices at
  residential four-year universities with 5,000+ undergraduates that are
  measured on student involvement.*
  - Outwardly perceivable: a Dean of Students / Student Life org chart with a
    named engagement or involvement office; an existing CampusGroups /
    Anthology / Modern Campus contract; a published involvement or belonging
    goal.
  - Homogeneous need: judged on participation and its link to retention;
    owe an annual engagement report upward; watching a post-pandemic campus
    empty out.
  - Findable: NASPA / ACUI conferences; peer-school reference; the campus
    that ran the pilot.
  - Next segment, for expansion: VP Student Life / Dean of Students as the
    engagement-measurement consolidator — see Section 7.
  - (Cornell is the design partner and pilot site.)
- Archetype (decision-maker): **Priya Anand**, Director of Student Engagement
  at a 13,000-student university. She's measured on involvement and on
  first-year belonging, and her campus goes quiet by 6pm. She has CampusGroups,
  but it only tells her who *scanned into an official event* — not who has
  actually found their people. Every spring she owes the VP an engagement
  report she assembles from event scans and a low-response survey.

## Section 4 · Use case / customer ecosystem (2 slides)

**Slide 5 · Student use case (B2C side) — Sarah**
- (a) Awareness: an RA seeds a floor anchor at the first hall meeting; a club
  officer and the CS 2110 SI leader each seed one ("put it in Loop"). Sarah
  sees a crew forming around something she already wanted to do.
- (b) Acquisition: free; installs, verifies with .edu, a few taps; puts a
  small **refundable stake** on her first seat.
- (c) Job to be done: **"Have people to do things with — and actually go."**
  She types "want to go to the CS club stuff" (or "study for 2110", or "find a
  gym crew"). It joins an anchor near quorum; it fires → "It's on. Thu 7pm.
  Your seat's held. 4 others in." The stake and the fixed slot mean she
  actually shows. Same crew the next week. By week three she's the one texting
  them. Benefit: *a standing reason to be on campus, with people who know her.*
- Assumptions to flag: an RA/officer/SI leader seeds; a crew reaches quorum in
  the first weeks; the stake holds attendance; ≥40% of fired crews reach a
  third meeting.

**Slide 6 · Institution ecosystem (B2B side)**
| Role | Job title (single hypothesis) | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Initiator | Student Engagement Coordinator | Becomes aware from a peer's talk at NASPA/ACUI, or from the RA/office that ran the pilot |
| Decider | Director of Student Engagement / Campus Activities | JTBD: **"Grow involvement and prove it, on a campus that's emptying out — with the staff I have."** |
| Buyer | Same director, on a departmental PO under the campus bid threshold (typically $10–25K) | Facilitation licence; no RFP |
| End user | First-year students (the crews) + the coordinator who reads the engagement dashboard | Coordinator JTBD: **"See who's actually participating, from week one — not in next year's survey."** |
- Expansion buyer (not this sale): VP Student Life / Dean of Students, who buys
  the campus-wide engagement measurement — Section 7.

## Section 5 · Value proposition canvases (2 slides)

**Slide 7 · Student side**
- *Customer jobs:* find people; have things to do; actually go instead of
  meaning to; feel like campus is theirs.
- *Pains:* going alone is uncomfortable; plans need a planner and fizzle;
  it's week three and nothing has stuck; the campus feels anonymous.
- *Gains:* a standing crew; a real reason to show up; no organizing;
  same faces again.
- *Products & services:* intent → anchor → fires at quorum → recurring crew;
  the stake + fixed weekly slot; facts-only card (time, place, walk);
  quorum-gated "4 of 8 in"; event- and club-seeded anchors from the campus
  calendar feed.
- *Pain relievers:* ownerless so it never dies with a host; no feed, no
  profiles, no decline grid; the crew is *who you go with*, so nothing is done
  alone; the stake means it actually happens.
- *Gain creators:* repetition by default; small (~8 seats, fires at 4–5);
  campus-gated, so the crew is nearby and verified.

**Slide 8 · Institution side (student-engagement director)**
- *Customer jobs:* grow student involvement; keep students on campus; prove
  engagement to the VP; catch disengaged first-years before they drift.
- *Pains:* the only engagement data is NSSE (annual, self-report, low
  response) and CampusGroups event scans; no leading, per-student read; the
  campus empties out and she can't show she moved it.
- *Gains:* a continuous, behavioral engagement read; a leading first-50-days
  signal; a number for the VP that isn't a survey.
- *Products & services:* the facilitation layer (recurring crews across study,
  clubs, events) *plus* the **engagement index** it produces — who is in a
  recurring group, across how many domains, showing up, over time; disclosed,
  aggregate, opt-in.
- *Pain relievers:* measures behavior, not a survey; from week one, not next
  spring; earned by running the participation, so it's trustworthy and can't
  be bought off the shelf.
- *Gain creators:* the data compounds as more activities are facilitated;
  the crew-and-commitment layer no engagement-platform incumbent has —
  everyone lists what's happening; Loop makes students show up.

## Section 6 · Business model canvases (2 slides)

**Slide 9 · Student side** (free / refundable stake; this side produces the signal)
- CS: first-years at residential four-years.
- VP: a recurring crew to show up with for the things that make campus worth
  being at.
- CH: RA seeds the floor; club officer and SI leader seed the section; .edu
  sign-up; the campus Localist calendar feed as anchors.
- CR: automated, quiet; no host; the stake + reminders do the nudging.
- R$: No direct revenue — the student side is **free by design**; the platform
  earns on the institution side (two-sided model: students generate the
  engagement, the institution buys the measurement). The refundable stake is a
  commitment device, returned on attendance — not a revenue stream.
- KR: intent→anchor→fire engine; the stake/commitment stack; per-campus
  density; the engagement data.
- KA: launch ops in the first weeks of term; moderation of the allowlist;
  crisis-routing protocol for the intent box.
- KP: RAs, club officers, SI leaders; campus activities; calendar owners.
- C$: push (not SMS); LLM parse ≈ $0.10/student/yr; trust & safety; stake
  payment processing.

**Slide 10 · Institution side** (paid — student-engagement office)
- CS: student-engagement / campus-activities offices at residential 5,000+
  undergrad universities (expand: VP Student Life / provost).
- VP: a live, behavioral measure of who's participating — earned by running
  the participation, not surveying it.
- CH: research pilot via IRB + FERPA studies-exception agreement (no
  procurement); coordinator → director referral; NASPA / ACUI; departmental
  purchase under the bid threshold.
- CR: coordinator self-serve launch; one customer-success person per ~10
  campuses; annual renewal against the office's own engagement numbers.
- R$: facilitation licence $6–15K/office (land); campus-wide engagement
  measurement to the $75–150K VP/provost band (expand); IES SBIR / student-
  life grants bridge; outcome pricing if the pilot links engagement to
  persistence.
- KR: the engagement index + disclosed/aggregate reporting; the commitment
  engine; the compounding cross-domain participation dataset; nightly roster
  integration that needs no IT.
- KA: one-semester instrumented pilot (anchors randomised to the commitment
  stack; engagement instrumented against persistence); build the measurement
  and disclosure surfaces; per-campus launches early in the term.
- KP: the engagement coordinator (champion); the IRB and registrar; NASPA/ACUI;
  Stellic (later, engagement-signal integration / OEM); a research partner.
- C$: no SOC 2 / full HECVAT until ~campus 5; tech COGS ~$2/MAU/yr;
  cost-to-serve inside the land ACV, with grants covering the gap.

## Section 7 · Evolution of the model (2–3 slides)

**Slide 11 · First version → now**
| | First version (August) | Now |
|---|---|---|
| Output | recurring study group *with an owner* | ownerless crew, ~8 seats, fires at quorum 4–5, with a stake |
| What it's for | passing a hard course | a reason to come to and stay on campus (study + clubs + events) |
| Student segment | "college students" at Cornell | first-years at residential four-years |
| Institution buyer | advising, via a Stellic tie-in | student-engagement / campus-activities office → VP Student Life |
| What the institution buys | a hidden "belonging signal" | a disclosed, behavioral **engagement measurement**, earned by facilitating participation |
| Anti-flake | none (hope) | a refundable stake + fixed ritual (borrowed from Timeleft) |
| Supply | intents only | intents + the campus calendar feed as anchors (never a listing) |
| Metric | joined a loop | reached a third meeting; a continuous engagement index |
| Entry | procurement | IRB research pilot, then a purchase under the bid threshold |

**Slide 11b · The engine and the expansion**
- **The engine is activity-agnostic.** Study, clubs, events — one mechanism
  (recurring, blind-matched, staked, fixed-ritual crew). The activity is the
  skin; the engine is the company.
- **Facilitation earns the measurement.** You can't survey engagement into
  existence; you measure it by being the thing students use to engage. Sell
  the facilitation to the offices that run activities; the engagement signal
  is the byproduct — and the compounding, hard-to-copy asset.
- **Expansion:** land in one engagement office ($6–15K) → the measurement
  spans study + clubs + events across the campus → the VP Student Life buys
  the campus engagement index at $75–150K. Sizing (`ANGLES.md`): ~$130M TAM,
  ~$60M SAM.
- **The line we don't cross:** ingest supply as anchors; own the crew and the
  commitment. The day Loop becomes "a better events app," the moat is gone.

**Slide 12 · The seven questions (iterating for attractiveness)**
1. *Switching costs* — strong once it's the engagement system of record: the
   measurement is continuous and earned, and the crews are the students' own
   social fabric.
2. *Recurring revenue* — annual licence; renewal tied to the engagement
   numbers the office already reports upward.
3. *Earn before you spend* — yes: research pilot costs no compliance; first
   licences clear under the bid threshold; SOC 2 deferred; grants bridge.
4. *Game-changing cost structure* — tech COGS ~$2/MAU/yr; launch labour is the
   campus's own RAs / officers / SI leaders.
5. *Others do the work* — students form the crews; staff seed; the campus
   calendar supplies events; the IRB and registrar carry the pilot.
6. *Scalability* — per campus, but each campus grows by adding activity
   domains and rising to the campus-wide engagement contract.
7. *Protection from competition* — the moat is the compounding engagement
   dataset and the crew-and-commitment layer, which a survey vendor or an
   events/involvement platform (CampusGroups, Anthology, Modern Campus) can't
   build because they never touch the behavior.

**Slide 13 · What changed because of the analysis (and what's still open)**
- Dropped: the hidden advising signal; "belonging" as the headline; selling to
  advising first; the study-only framing; Cornell as customer.
- Added: the crew-and-commitment layer across study/clubs/events; the stake +
  fixed ritual (Timeleft mechanics); **engagement measurement as the product,
  facilitation as how you earn it**; the student-engagement buyer; research-
  pilot entry.
- Still unvalidated (next step): one semester, one campus, one activity —
  do ≥40% of fired crews reach a third meeting, does the stake hold them, and
  does an engagement office recognise the read as something it would buy?
