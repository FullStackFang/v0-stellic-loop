## §2 · Business thesis + aspiration

**Business thesis** (one sentence; single offering · single segment · single benefit):

> **Loop's self-forming recurring study groups give the Supplemental Instruction director
> at a four-year residential university the session turnout their program is judged on.**

- *Offering* — self-forming (quorum-fired) recurring study groups.
- *Segment* — the Supplemental Instruction director at a four-year residential university.
- *Benefit* — session **turnout** (the single metric the director is accountable for) —
  stated in the *buyer's* terms, not the student's.
- No "and / or / if"; one clause; no second segment or second benefit smuggled in.

**Aspiration** (vision):

> **Every first-year has a group they return to each week — and the campus programs built
> to catch them are full instead of half-empty.**

> *Decision flag:* the benefit is deliberately the **buyer's** (turnout), not the student's,
> and it is a *single* benefit — the ICSI report / attendance-of-record is demoted to a
> supporting feature in §5, not a co-headline, or "single most important benefit" fails.
> The student's benefit surfaces in §4 (end-user JTBD) and as the delivery mechanism in §6.
> *(Earlier draft's "no student slips away unnoticed" was cut: it echoes the surveillance /
> covert-advising framing this model explicitly dropped — see §7d.)*

## §3 · Customer segment (1 slide)

**(a) Segment — Index-Card Test:**

> **Directors of academic-support / learning centers that run a paid Supplemental
> Instruction program at four-year residential universities with 5,000+ undergraduates.**

- *Outwardly perceivable:* a public SI/PLTL program page; an ICSI-trained supervisor; paid
  student SI leaders; an incumbent scheduling tool (TracCloud, WCONLINE, or AccuTrack).
- *Homogeneous need:* judged on SI-session attendance and DFW rates in gateway courses;
  owes a GPA-by-attendance-band + DFW report to accreditation (ICSI) and the provost.
- *Findable / acquirable the same way:* ICSI and CRLA member lists; the NCLCA/ICLCA
  conference; the ~1,500 institutions that have sent staff to ICSI training.
- *One segment, not a list:* the qualifiers narrow a single buyer type; they do not smuggle
  in res-life or advising (those are §7 expansion, not this segment).

**(b) Archetype — the decision-maker:**

> **Marcus Bell**, Director of the Academic Success Center at a 14,000-student public
> university. Runs 22 SI leaders across 30 gateway sections on a ~$90K wage budget. About
> **10%** of enrolled students ever attend a session; attendance is kept on paper sign-in
> sheets keyed into TracCloud by a work-study student; every spring he owes the provost a
> report tying attendance to grades. His sign-in system (AccuTrack) is being **sunset**, and
> IT wants it replaced by August.

*Single composite individual, conforms to the segment, and carries a live buying trigger
(the sunset).*

## §5 · Value Proposition Canvas (1 slide) — the center / director

**Customer profile (the director):**

- *Customer jobs:* fill the SI sessions the center already staffs; keep attendance of
  record; report GPA-by-attendance-band + DFW to ICSI and the provost; keep SI leaders from
  burning out on empty rooms; replace a sunsetting sign-in system before IT forces it.
- *Pains:* ~10% of enrolled students ever attend; paper sign-in sheets keyed by hand;
  sessions are admin-created calendar slots nobody committed to; the same students don't
  return; the spring report is assembled by hand; **turnout is the number they're judged on
  and can't move.**
- *Gains:* more students in the room; the *same* students returning; attendance that joins
  to the registrar automatically; a report the provost believes.

**Value map (Loop):**

- *Products & services:* quorum-fired recurring SI groups seeded by SI leaders; mutual
  check-in as attendance of record; SI-leader roster + timesheet export; ICSI-format report
  (enrollment, SI enrollment, GPA by attendance band, DFW); coordinator self-launch from a
  nightly roster file — no IT project.
- *Pain relievers:* replaces the sign-in ledger the center is losing anyway; ends paper and
  hand-keying; groups form from real student demand instead of an admin's guess at a time
  slot; the commitment stack converts one-time visitors into regulars.
- *Gain creators:* the **"10% → 40% turnout"** line; dose–response evidence (10+ sessions
  vs 1–2: **+29pp A/B, −26pp DFW**) the director can cite to the provost; a **"reached a
  third meeting"** rate as a new number they own.

*Fit:* every pain has a reliever, every gain a creator, and **all of it is the director's** —
no student jobs/pains leak into this canvas.

> *Single-offering guardrail:* the one offering is **self-forming recurring SI groups**. The
> check-in, roster/timesheet export, and ICSI report are **features / pain-relievers of that
> one offering**, not a second product — and the dominant *gain creator* is **turnout**, kept
> aligned to the §2 thesis benefit. Presenting turnout and the ICSI report as two co-equal
> deliverables would break "single most important benefit." The student-facing app (intent →
> anchor → fire, the commitment stack) is the **delivery mechanism** — it re-enters the model
> as a Channel / Key Resource in §6, never as its own canvas.

## §7 · Evolution of the model (2–3 slides)

**7a · First version → now**

| | First version (August) | Now |
|---|---|---|
| Who pays | advising / retention office, via a vague "belonging signal" | **academic-support (SI) director**, $6–15K, under the bid line |
| Offering to the buyer | a belonging leading-indicator dashboard | SI sessions students keep attending + attendance of record + the ICSI report |
| Output to the student | a recurring group *with an owner* (dies if the host leaves) | ownerless anchor, ~8 seats, fires at quorum 4–5, commitment stack |
| Segment | "college students at Cornell" | first-years in gateway STEM courses; **buyer** = SI centers at 4-yr residential universities |
| The signal | hidden from students | disclosed, opt-in, binary, n≥10 aggregates; harvested from the workflow, not pitched |
| Entry | procurement / sell-the-signal | IRB **research pilot**, then a purchase under the bid threshold |
| Metric | "joined a loop" | **reached a third meeting → DFW**, instrumented |

**7b · The seven questions (iterating for attractiveness)**

1. **Switching costs** — weak → strong once two offices share it: attendance of record and
   the ICSI report make Loop the ledger; the cross-office indicator makes it the system of
   record for peer participation.
2. **Recurring revenue** — annual license per center; renewal tied to the center's own
   attendance numbers; consolidation raises it to the provost.
3. **Earn before you spend** — yes: the research pilot costs no compliance; first licenses
   clear under the bid threshold; SOC 2 / full HECVAT deferred to ~campus 5; IES SBIR bridges.
4. **Game-changing cost structure** — tech COGS ~$2/MAU/yr; launch labor is the center's
   *paid* SI leaders. *Honest caveat:* cost-to-serve ~$8–15K/campus is **at or above** the
   land ACV, so the land deal is a wedge, not a standalone profit — the cost advantage only
   compounds across the §7 multi-office expansion (see §6, §8).
5. **Others do the work** — students form the groups; SI leaders seed; the campus calendar
   (Localist) feed supplies event anchors; the IRB and registrar carry the pilot; Stellic's
   contract carries the compliance paper later.
6. **Scalability** — one 10-day launch window per campus per year, *but* each campus grows
   5–10× by adding offices, so growth is not purely campus count.
7. **Protection from competition** — no IP; the moat is the de-identified third-meeting →
   DFW dataset across campuses and the cross-office view no single-office vendor (TracCloud,
   Roompact, Mentor Collective, Stellic Care) can assemble.

**7c · Why the beachhead is small on purpose (the expansion that makes it a company)**

- Four offices on every campus pay for the *same* job — getting students into small
  recurring groups: the tutoring center (SI), housing (RA programs), orientation (first-year
  cohorts), wellbeing (connectedness). Each has its own small budget; none can see the
  others.
- Loop enters through the tutoring center (cheapest, fastest sale), then adds offices **on
  the same campus** before adding campuses. The **provost** buys the consolidated layer at
  **$75–150K** — EAB's band — because only the cross-office view answers *"is this student in
  any recurring group?"*
- Scale (one line — the rubric never asks for a sizing slide, so keep it to this): the
  multi-office layer is a ~$60M SAM across ~600 publics and mid-privates, a 3-year path to
  ~$1–1.5M ARR at 10–15 full accounts. **Outcome pricing** (per retained student, à la
  InsideTrack) opens up *only after* the pilot proves a causal effect — it is an expansion
  lever, which is why it lives here and not in the §6 canvas.

**7d · What changed, and what is still open**

- *Dropped:* Cornell as the customer; the hidden signal; "belonging" as the headline;
  selling the signal to advising as the first deal.
- *Added:* the tutoring-center wedge and its workflow (attendance of record, ICSI report);
  the commitment stack; deep-then-wide expansion to the provost; calendar events + a curated
  academic-support map as supply; research-pilot entry.
- *Still unvalidated (the next step):* one semester at a pilot campus, anchors randomised to
  the commitment stack — do **≥40% of fired loops reach a third meeting**, and does
  instrumented attendance **move DFW**?

## §8 · Adversarial stress-test findings & fixes

*Three parallel red-teams — rubric conformance, VC viability, canvas consistency.*
*`[applied]` = already folded into the body above. `[disclose]` = put an on-slide caveat.*
*`[your call]` = a decision only you can make before building slides.*

**Headline (VC teardown).** As written, a skeptical seed VC reads this as *a research project
wrapped around a feature* more than a fundable venture — the core product behavior is
unmeasured and the venture-scale value sits entirely in an unproven expansion. For this
*course* deliverable that is survivable — the rubric explicitly rewards honestly-flagged
hypotheses — **but only if you name the risks rather than paper over them.** The fixes below
are mostly about disclosure, not rescue.

**1 · The load-bearing unknown — the third meeting** `[disclose]`
Everything the director buys (turnout, attendance, the DFW signal) is downstream of one
unmeasured behavior: do quorum-fired, ownerless, free groups recur to a 3rd/4th meeting at
~40%? Nobody has measured it, and the commitment-stack effect sizes (+4–12pp each) come from
domains with a *personal* payoff (gym, savings, vaccines), not "walk across campus to meet
near-strangers." → Put the one-semester RCT **on the slide**, and quote every downstream
number (turnout, DFW, ARR) as **conditional** on it clearing ~40%. (Now assumption #1 in §9.)

**2 · The pilot-site trap** `[disclose]` — *the sharpest new finding*
The pilot runs at Cornell (dense dorms, no retention headroom). The paying customer is
regional publics / commuter campuses where that density doesn't exist. **A successful Cornell
pilot can fail to de-risk the business.** → Name Cornell as *design partner*, and state that
external validity needs a second, representative campus. (Now assumption #6 in §9.)

**3 · Two internal contradictions a grader/VC will catch** `[your call]`
These aren't just risks — they're inconsistencies. Pick a lane before you build:
- *Attendance-of-record vs. "defer compliance to campus 5."* If Loop is the system of record
  it holds FERPA education records → DPA, SOC 2, SSO **now**. Either (a) Loop **is** the
  attendance ledger (then compliance is a real §6 cost, not deferred), or (b) Loop is
  **turnout-only** and rides the incumbent's ledger (then "attendance of record" leaves the
  value prop). You can't be both the ledger and compliance-light.
- *"Ownerless so it runs free" vs. "paid SI leaders seed every group."* You can't claim both
  zero-labor economics and staff-seeded reliability. Decide whether seeding is required; if it
  is, cost it into cost-to-serve.

**4 · Unit economics: break-even-to-underwater at land** `[applied]` `[disclose]`
Cost-to-serve $8–15K vs. ACV $6–15K ≈ −$9K/campus at the bad corner; "grants cover the gap"
is an admission, not a resolution. → §6 Cost Structure now states the tension and frames land
as a **wedge that only profits via §7 expansion**; grants demoted to a financing bridge. Be
ready to say aloud: *the model does not stand on the land deal alone.*

**5 · No IP moat; incumbent build-vs-buy** `[disclose]`
TracCloud / WCONLINE / EAB / Stellic Care already own the roster, scheduling, attendance,
SSO, compliance — and the users. "Recurring groups" is a feature they can ship in a sprint,
and the "outcomes dataset" moat **doesn't exist yet** (EAB already runs that business at
scale). → State plainly: *no IP; the only defensible asset is a validated outcomes dataset we
don't yet have* — and make the case for **why the incumbent doesn't do it first** (the current
docs don't actually make it).

**6 · Rubric conformance — the structural hits are already resolved** `[applied]`
The rubric red-team's biggest deductions (two theses, two segments, two VPCs, two BMCs, two
archetypes) were all symptoms of the **two-sided** origin of `PRESENTATION.md`. This spine is
single-sided from the start, so those collapse. Confirmed/applied here:
- Thesis rewritten **buyer-framed**, single benefit (turnout), no run-on (§2). *(Matches the
  red-team's own strongest draft.)*
- §4 de-hedged: one decider title, one initiator awareness path, decider JTBD cut to one job.
  **End user confirmed = SI leader** (a job title inside the buying org; a student has none);
  student named once as the beneficiary, kept out of the four-role map.
- **Single offering** named ("self-forming recurring SI groups"); check-in / roster / ICSI
  report demoted to features (§5), so "single most important benefit" holds.
- All expansion (res life / new-student / provost / TAM-SAM-SOM / outcome pricing) confined
  to §7; §6 Revenue Streams is the one land line. Archetype = Marcus Bell only.

**7 · Timing / execution risks to keep in your pocket** `[disclose]`
- One ~10-day acquisition window per campus per year (late August). Miss orientation and the
  first-50-days thesis is dead for 12 months.
- The live product is a single `index.html` on in-memory state; SSO / SIS feed / SOC 2 /
  HECVAT / WCAG 2.1 AA don't exist, and several signature mechanics (timed quorum countdown,
  color-only status, geo-only check-in) fail the accessibility gates procurement now enforces.
- No evident higher-ed enterprise seller on the team — the scarcest resource in this market.
  Stellic has signed nothing; a hackathon relationship is not a partnership.

**Still your call before slides:** (a) end user = SI leader vs. student — §4; (b) resolve the
two contradictions in finding 3; (c) whether to keep any sizing at all in §7 (now one line).

## §9 · Open assumptions to declare on the deck

*The course accepts reasoned hypotheses without collected evidence — but rewards flagging
them. Put a short "assumptions" line under the slides that lean on these:*

1. **The third meeting.** ≥40% of fired loops recur to a 3rd/4th session (the single biggest
   unknown; the whole B2B value rides on it).
2. **Turnout lift is causal.** The commitment stack moves turnout from ~10% toward ~40% —
   currently supported by analogous studies (Lund, Swarthmore, Milkman), not Loop's own data.
3. **The director is a real buyer.** Discretionary $6–15K, pain acute enough to buy this
   term, and the sunsetting sign-in system is a live trigger.
4. **Free-tier student adoption.** Enough students in a 200-seat course drop an intent within
   week one for a quorum to form.
5. **The expand is reachable.** A second office on the same campus buys, and the provost
   consolidation ($75–150K) is a destination, not a fantasy.
6. **External validity.** A pilot at dense-dorm Cornell transfers to the commuter /
   regional-public campuses that are the *actual* buyers — where the residential density Loop
   leans on doesn't exist. (Name Cornell as *design partner*, not proof of the market.)
