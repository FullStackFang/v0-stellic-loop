## The pattern nobody wants to hear

Every buyer of "belonging" pays four or low-five figures. Six angles, six
ceilings: $702 (Donut), $1,999–16K (learning center), $12K (Togetherall),
$15K (res life), $50K (FYE, counseling), $5/student (online). The only
places in this entire research where real money moves are:

1. **Clinical-adjacent services** — TimelyCare $436K/yr at JMU ✓, Mantra
   $8.75M/5yr at MinnState ✓. Regulated, and a licensed-provider business.
2. **Enterprise student-success suites** — EAB Navigate $53–165K/yr ✓.
   Systems of record, sold to provosts, 8-year contracts.
3. **Outcome-priced human coaching** — InsideTrack $500/student/semester ✓,
   with an NBER RCT showing +5.3pp retention.
4. **Paid consumer recurring dinners** — Timeleft €18M ARR, 150K monthly ~.
   The single revenue-scale "meet strangers, repeatedly" business found.

Belonging *tooling* is priced like a feature. Belonging *outcomes* and
belonging *services* are priced like a business.

## Two angles that could actually be venture-scale

### F. The campus-wide recurring-group layer, priced at the provost

The six offices above all buy the *same mechanic* for different rooms.
Nobody sells it across them. Land in one (learning center: fastest, cheapest,
seeds the evidence), then sell the *sum* of the lines to the provost or
VP Student Affairs as one platform: SI groups + floor groups + orientation
cohorts + connectedness programming, one indicator across all of them.

- Per-campus: $6–15K + $10–20K + $35–50K + $25–60K ≈ **$75–150K ACV** at
  the provost level — EAB's band, which the campus already pays.
- TAM: ~1,300 institutions with ≥500 beds × $100K ≈ **$130M**; SAM
  (publics + mid-privates with <85% retention, ~600) ≈ **$60M**.
- Why it's not just "the sum of small things": the indicator only means
  something if it sees every recurring group a student is in. One office's
  view is a feature; the cross-office view is the system of record for
  peer participation — the thing Stellic Care can't build because it has
  no groups.
- What it needs: the learning-center land (year 1), a second office on the
  same campus (year 2), the instrumented pilot proving third-meeting →
  DFW. Then the provost sale is a consolidation of lines they already fund.
- Escape hatch on price: **outcome pricing.** InsideTrack gets $500/student
  because it proves retention. If the IV-designed RCT shows a causal DFW
  effect, price per retained student, not per seat.

### G. Paid consumer, campus as the launch density

The uncomfortable one. Timeleft is the only company in this research that
turned recurring stranger-groups into real revenue — by charging. Payment
is also the strongest known anti-flake device, which is Loop's biggest risk.

- Model: Loop free to form; a small paid tier (~$5–8/mo ~) for the
  recurring dinner/activity loops, Timeleft-style fixed weekly ritual.
  Study loops stay free (that's the institutional product).
- Campus is the density seed (every user within a mile), then Collegetown,
  then cities — the path the Regroup spinoff already gestures at.
- TAM: 15M US undergrads × 5% paying × $70/yr ≈ **$50M** on campus alone;
  Timeleft's numbers show the off-campus market is larger.
- Against: 20-year-olds' willingness to pay is untested; every campus
  social app has died; it abandons the institution story entirely. But it
  is the only model here with a proven revenue-scale comp.

## Recommendation

**Run F.** It keeps everything already built (the mechanic, the staff
lenses, the indicator, the research narrative), starts with the wedge
already chosen (learning center, 1–3 months, no compliance floor), and has
a venture-sized destination the point-solution version lacks. It is also
the only angle where Loop's multi-office design — RA lens, TA lens, advisor
lens — stops being scope creep and becomes the product.

Keep G as a controlled experiment, not a strategy: one paid dinner loop on
one campus, to learn whether payment fixes the third meeting. If it does,
that result improves F too.

Drop C (counseling), D (online), E (employers) as first markets. Keep D as
the year-3 expand: WGU Labs and ASU ScaleU both run paid pilots, and one
logo is 200K students.

## The number that decides everything, restated

None of the six angles change the dependency: a recurring group that
survives to a third meeting. F's provost price is only real if the
indicator predicts something; G's subscription is only real if people show
up. The one-semester instrumented pilot is the first move under every angle.
